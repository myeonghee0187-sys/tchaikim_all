How Tchai Kim thinks about hanbok

Everyday

Hanbok wasn’t always something set aside
for holidays and ceremonies.
Long before it became formalwear,
it was simply what people reached for every morning —
to work in, to walk in, to move through an ordinary day.
It belonged to daily life, not to display.

Forward

Somewhere along the way, hanbok drifted from
that everyday role into something reserved
for special occasions only.
Tchai Kim brings it back to where it started,
reshaping the silhouette so it can be worn
the way it was always meant to be —
with ease, and with room to move.

Seasons

Long before it shaped fashion,
color in Korea followed the seasons.
The soft green of new spring leaves,
the deep red of summer’s fullest bloom,
the warm gold of autumn’s last harvest —
each color carried a season within it,
long before it ever touched a piece of cloth.

Obangsaek

Blue, red, yellow, white, and black —
Korea’s five directional colors have carried
meaning for centuries, each tied to a direction,
an element, a way of seeing the world.
Tchai Kim carries that same palette forward,
letting it move quietly through every piece we make.

Woven

Some things can’t be added after the fact.
The color in our fabric isn’t printed on the surface —
it’s woven into the thread itself,
present from the very first stitch.
What you see is what the fabric has always been,
from the inside out.
